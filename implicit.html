Implicit Bias: Educational Resources

This page explores the concept of implicit bias and its impact on our daily lives.

A Brief Overview of Implicit Bias and Intersectionality:

Implicit bias refers to the attitudes or stereotypes that trigger our unconscious attitudes and decisions related to a specific social group.

Intersectionality examines how various social identities such as race, gender, and class interact to create an individual's unique experiences.

Implicit Bias Tests

The Gender-Career Implicit Association Test

Created by researchers at Harvard University, the Gender-Career IAT is an IBT designed to measure implicit biases related to gender and career. The test tasked the user with associating "female" and "male" names with "career"-related and "family"-related phrases, switching the associations at different stages of the test. The overall results indicated that more users associated female names with family-related phrases and male names with career-related phrases.

The Race Implicit Association Test

Also developed by researchers at Harvard University, the Race IAT measures implicit biases related to race and ethnicity. Similar to the Gender-Career IAT, the Race IAT tasked users with associating black and white faces with either being good or bad, and switching the associations as the test progressed. The results at the end indicated that more users were quicker to associate white faces as being "good" than black faces and vice versa.

After taking these tests, it may be important to consider our own implicit biases and assumptions. At the same time, there is not a clear explanation of how these tests indicate one's assumptions and how exactly they are measuring implicit biases. The structure of the IAT is not a good predictor of one's behavior or implicit biases, as there are many explanations why one "scores" the way they do, including reaction time, time of day, internet stability and other external factors. Ultimately, it is not fair to assume an individual's implicit biases based on their IAT results.

Intersectionality

As defined by Patricia Hill Collins (2016), Intersectionality refers to the way of understanding and examining the interactions between people, humanity, and the world. Various aspects of identity work together to shape organizational power.

A transcript from Ben Shapiro's podcast and an article written by Dr. Robin DiAngelo share varying viewpoints about the concept of intersectionality. Shapiro argues that intersectionality has become a system of ranking how marginalized individuals with certain identities are, while DiAngelo emphasizes the idea that white people are often uncomfortable with conversations surrounding race and racism. She argues that white people may act defensively as a mechanism to approach such conversations.

The two, Ben Shapiro and Dr. Robin DiAngelo, offer opposite stances on the topic of intersectionality. DiAngelo's discussion on white people acting defensively is interesting after reviewing Shapiro's defensive reflection on intersectionality. It's important to approach conversations about racism productively by being willing to listen and learn. We must recognize our own assumptions and acknowledge how intersectionality plays into humanity as a whole.
